### “Accessibility is testability”

[John Hill](https://www.linkedin.com/in/linkedjohnhill/)

[Accessibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accessibility) is more than a compliance requirement—it’s a powerful tool for creating resilient, reliable automated tests. Using NASA’s Open Mission Control Technologies ([Open MCT](https://github.com/nasa/openmct)) as a case study, this session will demonstrate how implementing accessibility standards not only improves usability but also strengthens test automation workflows.

Attendees will see real-world examples of how traditional locators in [Playwright](https://playwright.dev/) can lead to brittle, flaky tests and how accessibility-driven development resolves these challenges. Through live demonstrations, including the use of a screen reader, we’ll explore the practical impact of semantic HTML, [ARIA](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Accessibility/ARIA) attributes, and focus management on both user experience and test stability.

This session will provide actionable insights into incorporating accessibility standards into your applications, leveraging Playwright to write robust tests, and reducing test flakiness—all while making your applications more inclusive and user-friendly.

#### Bio

As a UIDX Web Test Frameworks engineer at [Crowdstrike](https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/), [John Hill](https://www.linkedin.com/in/linkedjohnhill/) maintains web test frameworks for over 900 engineers. With over 14 years of experience, he has seen web technologies evolve from [Adobe Flash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adobe_Flash) to today's advanced [JavaScript](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JavaScript) frameworks.

John’s expertise spans Web Test Automation, Performance Engineering, and Test Automation Architecture, and his contributions to Open Source projects like [Jenkins](https://www.jenkins.io/), [Ansible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansible_(software)) and Open MCT showcase his dedication to the community.
